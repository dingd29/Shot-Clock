The behavior

Watch teams hurry—then relax.

The line shows how long the offense uses after gaining the ball near the end of a quarter. The response is gradual, not a knife-edge discontinuity.

At 24–31 seconds there is usually no extra trip to create. From roughly 34 seconds, offenses hurry; beyond 40 seconds they can relax because enough time remains anyway.

The mechanism

Two sides of the same ledger.

The extra possession is valuable. So is the current action a team abandons early. The project measures both.

Actual possession lengths vary. That variance blurs who gets the last handover and shrinks the empirical end-period sawtooth to 0.035–0.041 points—about one-twelfth of the rigid textbook benchmark.

What was built

A measurement system, not one model.

Every layer has an audit trail, generated tables, tests, and explicit failure modes.

Reconstruct the clock

A deterministic event-state machine applies 24- and 14-second resets, handles live and dead rebounds, repairs feed timestamps, and leaves uncertain clocks missing.

Validate independently

96.0% of shots receive a usable clock. Against NBA's untouched player-by-bucket splits, FGA per game reaches R² 0.973.

Chain full possessions

Offensive rebounds remain inside the same possession, allowing the model to price retained-ball and second-chance value.

Estimate continuation value

A historical curve estimates what an offense still has available at every reconstructed second, with chronological splits and team-game nulls.

Stress-test claims

Preregistrations, held-out seasons, foul exercises, public tracking, robustness variants, null results, and withdrawn claims stay visible.
