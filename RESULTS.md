# Results index

This is the public index for the project's major findings. It separates replicated results,
exploratory descriptions, null results, and withdrawn claims. Exact estimates, specification
tables, and caveats live in [reports/findings.md](reports/findings.md); model construction and
validation live in [METHODOLOGY.md](METHODOLOGY.md).

## Status key

- **Validated:** checked against an independent external target.
- **Held out:** designed on 2015-16 to 2021-22 and evaluated on 2022-23 to 2023-24.
- **Replicated:** same directional result appears across seasons or a separate window.
- **Exploratory:** design and historical result were developed together.
- **Null:** the test did not support the proposed relationship.
- **Withdrawn:** an earlier claim failed a later identification or inference check.

## 1. Reconstruction and validation

The reconstruction is a deterministic event-state machine with explicit reset rules, confidence
flags, and a calibration based only on shot-clock violations. The NBA aggregate comparison is a
separate holdout.

Key artifacts:

- `reports/` validation tables are generated by `make validate`.
- Reconstruction rules and failure modes: [METHODOLOGY.md §2-4](METHODOLOGY.md#2-shot-clock-reconstruction).
- Golden event sequences and invariants: `tests/test_reconstruct.py`, `tests/test_validate.py`.

## 2. Efficiency, selection, and possession value

The continuous curve recovers information lost inside the NBA's six buckets. Efficiency declines
late, but the curve conditions on possessions that survived to each second. That creates adverse
selection: a possession alive at five seconds is one where earlier actions did not produce a shot.

The project therefore treats the raw curve as description and uses an optimal-stopping framing for
the decision question. A shot is compared with the estimated value of continuing at that second.
The curve's *shape* is the defensible object; its absolute decision threshold depends on an
unidentified quantile choice and on selection into continuation.

## 3. Offensive rebounds and second chances

A miss can preserve the possession. Ignoring this option understated shot value, especially late
in the clock where desperate interior attempts are retained unusually often. The corrected
valuation adds the expected second-chance value to every shot and chains all chances belonging to
the same possession.

## 4. The 2018-19 rule change

The 14-second reset clearly shortened offensive-rebound chances. The project does **not** claim a
defensible scoring effect. With only nine season clusters, the point estimate fails the exact wild
cluster bootstrap, and a play-by-play timestamp convention changed primarily on the treated arm
one season before the rule.

## 6. The 2-for-1 and end-of-period value

Teams respond strongly to the 2-for-1 window, but its payoff is bounded near zero. The key result
is why: real possession durations are distributed broadly around 13 seconds, so good and bad
handover moments blur within two trips. The large sawtooth implied by a fixed-duration model is not
present in the data.

## 7. Team continuation profiles

Team analysis keeps outcome, timing style, continuation capability, and deviation separate.
Below-curve exposure is positively associated with offense because strong teams preserve a more
valuable option. The useful screen is therefore a conjunction—poor outcome plus high exposure—not
a league-wide waste ranking.

Houston and Orlando enter the 2022-24 review queue. Context decomposition changes their readings:
Orlando's result is largely its attempt mix, while Houston remains high after matching shot family,
clock phase, and possession origin. Neither result proves that waiting would have produced a better
shot on a specific possession.

## 8. Mechanism probes

The mechanism work tests four explanations: shot/possession context, roster versus team
environment, game-state pressure, and short-run sequencing. Context and close-game creation are
the strongest descriptive paths. Roster continuity is mixed. Sequencing is substantively tiny.

## 9. Player grades and league projection

Shot Quality Grade separates selection from making using out-of-sample expected points. It cannot
observe defender distance and should be read as shot-selection context, not a complete measure of
shot difficulty.

The 2026-27 league projection is a separate, pre-registered application built from DARKO player
impact, playing-time assumptions, SRS calibration, and simulation. Its original frozen projection
and corrected version are both retained so changes remain auditable.
